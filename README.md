## `01` — The way I see engineering

AI is fascinating. Engineering it well is harder.

I'm an AI engineer with a software development background. I build and explore systems that bring together language models, retrieval, tools, data, and application logic.

What interests me most is everything that makes an AI application dependable: understanding the request, selecting the right context, coordinating actions, validating outputs, and handling the cases where things don't go as planned.

I don't look at an AI feature as just a model call. I look at the **system around it**.

## `03` — Problems that make me think

Not just features I have built or explored — problem spaces that have shaped how I approach AI engineering.

<details>
<summary><strong>01 · Financial intelligence systems</strong></summary>

How do you build AI-assisted financial analysis that is structured, evidence-backed, and constrained by deterministic checks?

This involves thinking about domain rules, financial reasoning, data validation, risk signals, and keeping decision support explainable.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>02 · Cross-domain retrieval</strong></summary>

How does a system understand which domain a question belongs to, retrieve the right information, and avoid mixing context from unrelated sources?

This leads into query understanding, retrieval strategy, domain selection, context quality, and answer grounding.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>03 · Agentic testing</strong></summary>

How can we evaluate an external AI application when its internal implementation is unknown?

I am interested in generated test scenarios, golden datasets, multi-turn conversations, and repeatable evaluation workflows.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>04 · Multilingual and voice AI</strong></summary>

How do language detection, speech recognition, and translation work together when accuracy and latency both matter?

This problem space brings together language classification, ASR pipelines, model behaviour, and performance trade-offs.

</details>

## `05` — My default way of thinking

```text
START WITH THE PROBLEM
        │
        ▼
Understand the request, data & constraints
        │
        ▼
Design the system & define boundaries
        │
        ▼
Choose what should be code, retrieval or AI
        │
        ▼
Build, integrate and validate
        │
        ▼
Test failure cases — not just the happy path
        │
        ▼
Measure. Learn. Improve.
```

A framework is a tool, not an engineering decision. I care about understanding trade-offs, choosing the right abstraction, and knowing why a system behaves the way it does.
